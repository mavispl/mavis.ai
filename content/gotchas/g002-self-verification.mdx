---
id: g002
title: Don't use a model's self-assessment as a verification control
slug: self-verification
category: Testing & Evaluation
severity: high
whatGoesWrong: >
  A pipeline asks the model to check its own work, the model agrees with itself, and
  the agreement is recorded as validation. Errors pass review with a confidence score
  attached, which makes them harder to catch downstream than if no check had run.
whyItHappens: >
  The original answer sits in the context with its original framing, so it dominates
  the distribution. The likeliest continuation of a confident answer plus "is this
  correct?" is agreement. The model has no privileged access to its own reliability —
  "yes, I'm sure" is produced by the same process that produced the claim.
howToDetect: >
  Look for any quality gate whose only evidence is model output about model output.
  Measure it: take twenty cases you know are wrong and see how many the self-check
  catches. Teams are routinely surprised by how close to zero this is.
fix: >
  Verification needs an external source of truth — a test suite, a type checker, a
  source document, a tool that can disagree. Where a second model pass genuinely helps,
  strip the original framing so the claim is evaluated on its own, not as "your answer".
relatedLessons: [w1.m01.b03, w1.m01.b06]
sources:
  - url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.04664
    title: Why Language Models Hallucinate
    publisher: Kalai et al., arXiv
    accessedOn: 2026-09-04
verifiedOn: 2026-09-04
---

Self-review is useful as a *drafting* step and worthless as a *control*. The distinction
is whether anything outside the model can contradict the result.
